This page is a short account of Randall Degges, taken from his own posts and from public event pages. Nothing here is filled in from memory. If a date or a detail is incomplete, the page says so.


## How he got started

He has written that he started coding because he wanted to hack video games. The family's first computer came with [Wolfenstein 3D](https://www.rdegges.com/2011/i-want-to-be-a-renaissance-man/) and Tyrant. He and his brother played Diablo II and long Starcraft matches. He says he had no technical contacts and no guidance, and learned from tutorials and forums. He also says his real learning started the day he [installed Linux](https://www.rdegges.com/2012/how-i-learned-to-program/), after he had already picked up computers through MS-DOS games.

In high school he gave his first technical presentation, to the computer club, on one-time pad encryption, with a C implementation. That talk is at the bottom of the timeline. He did not record the year.

He has written that he has been writing code every day [since he was 12](https://www.rdegges.com/2014/moving-on/), and that by February 2014 he had been a developer for 13 years. In another post from 2014 he put the same start at ["maybe 12."](https://www.rdegges.com/2014/tech-marketing-and-spam/) University started in [2006](https://www.rdegges.com/2012/new-habit-100-carbs/). He studied computer science, [dropped out after his second year](https://www.rdegges.com/2013/the-positive-programmer/), and then started working in the field. He does not name the school.


## What he works on

[Snyk's contributor page](https://snyk.io/contributors/randall-degges/) and his [Sessionize profile](https://sessionize.com/rdegges/) both list him as VP of AI Engineering and Developer Relations at Snyk. Those bios say he builds AI-powered security tooling and leads developer relations and community. His [GitHub profile](https://github.com/rdegges) lists Snyk, and its location field says "Bay Area | Bend, Oregon." Sessionize lists Bend, Oregon. Other Snyk and conference pages still use older titles, including Head of Developer and Security Relations and Senior Director of Developer Relations. The title above is the one on the contributor page and on Sessionize, both read in November 2026.

The path there, from his posts and from company pages:

- Telephony, starting roughly five years before February 2014. In [Moving On](https://www.rdegges.com/2014/moving-on/) he describes building PBX systems, phone and SMS applications, libraries, and APIs, then getting tired of telephony and wanting to work on developer tools.
- [OpenCNAM](https://www.rdegges.com/2014/my-startup-a-retrospective/), a Caller ID API he built with Mike and Chris (he does not give their last names). He describes a weekend Django MVP, a later Flask rewrite, and his role as CTO. On February 3, 2014 he stepped back to board and advisory work.
- Stormpath's first developer evangelist, from that same week. He was still there in [December 2015](https://www.rdegges.com/2015/two-years-of-evangelism/), about to finish two years, and wrote that most of his time went into client libraries rather than travel.
- Okta. In 2017 the Stormpath team joined Okta. [Okta's Oktane page](https://www.okta.com/video/oktane17-appdev-the-future-of-the-okta-api-our-devex-and-api-access-management/) describes an acquisition and uses both February and March. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/03/06/okta-stormpath/), on March 6, 2017, described an acquihire rather than a full acquisition. By [August 21, 2017](https://developer.okta.com/blog/2017/08/21/what-is-developer-relations-at-okta) he was Lead Developer Advocate at Okta. By [January 28, 2019](https://developer.okta.com/blog/2019/01/28/developer-relations-pro-tips) Okta's developer blog called him Head of Developer Advocacy.
- Snyk. On [January 30, 2022](https://www.rdegges.com/2022/how-to-calculate-the-energy-consumption-of-a-mac/) he wrote about an impact hackathon at Snyk. That post does not give a job title.

He still publishes here. In [August 2024](https://www.rdegges.com/2024/im-writing-again/) he wrote that he was publishing again after more than two years, and that most of his writing in that stretch had gone into a private journal.


## What he is interested in

The through-line in his writing is building things: APIs, open source, and later authentication and security. His Sessionize profile says he now speaks and writes about where AI and security meet: shipping quickly without shipping vulnerabilities, and what secure development looks like when AI writes most of the code.

In January 2022 he called himself a sustainability nerd. He wrote that he lives in a home with solar power, drives an electric vehicle, tries to avoid single-use plastics, and wants a neutral or negative carbon footprint. He did not say where that home is. In August 2024 he wrote about private journaling.

In [May 2011](https://www.rdegges.com/2011/i-want-to-be-a-renaissance-man/) he wrote that he wanted to stop being good at only one thing. The interests he named then were skateboarding in elementary and middle school, video games, programming, and fitness (a change he dated to October 2009), plus personal development, organization, bodybuilding, music, travel, astronomy, business, entrepreneurship, and design. That is a 2011 list, not a current inventory.


## GitHub and notable work

His code is at [github.com/rdegges](https://github.com/rdegges). The account was created May 30, 2009. Work he has written about, or that his repositories document:

- **[The Heroku Hacker's Guide](https://www.rdegges.com/2012/my-heroku-book-is-finished/).** He announced the book finished on September 10, 2012, as a PDF and a paperback. The original site is still at [theherokuhackersguide.com](https://www.theherokuhackersguide.com/). His announcement does not call it the first Heroku book. An Okta colleague later did.
- **OpenCNAM.** The Caller ID API above. He published `opencnam.com` in the retrospective. That address now redirects away from the product he described, so the writeup is the record.
- **[ipify](https://www.ipify.org/).** A free public-IP API. In [January 2018](https://www.rdegges.com/2018/to-30-billion-and-beyond/) he wrote that he built it first in Node and later in Go, that it had passed 30 billion requests a month, and that he had passed ownership to the team at whoisxmlapi while still helping. The [ipify-api](https://github.com/rdegges/ipify-api) README still calls him the creator and still says over 30 billion requests a month. His Sessionize profile says over 100 billion. Those figures disagree, and this page does not pick one.
- **Flask-Stormpath and express-stormpath.** He wrote in 2015 that he built both. On GitHub he is the top contributor to [stormpath-flask](https://github.com/stormpath/stormpath-flask) and [express-stormpath](https://github.com/stormpath/express-stormpath). Both repositories are archived, and the package metadata names Stormpath, Inc. as the author.
- **[django-twilio](https://github.com/rdegges/django-twilio).** The README lists him as the author.
- Other public repositories of his that have drawn a lot of attention include [django-skel](https://github.com/rdegges/django-skel), [django-sslify](https://github.com/rdegges/django-sslify), [skele-cli](https://github.com/rdegges/skele-cli), and [simpleq](https://github.com/rdegges/simpleq). Star counts move. As of November 7, 2026, those four plus ipify-api and django-twilio were the most-starred public repositories on the account.

He has written and spoken about PASETO tokens. In an [October 17, 2019 Okta post](https://developer.okta.com/blog/2019/10/17/a-thorough-introduction-to-paseto) he credits the design to Scott Arciszewski. [paseto.io](https://paseto.io/) does not list him as an author, and none of his public repositories is a PASETO library.


## Talks

The timeline is a separate data file, `data/talks.json` in this site's repository, so it can grow without rewriting this page. Each entry keeps the pages it came from.

It is not a complete list. He has written that preparing a talk can take more than 40 hours, and for several years speaking was part of the job, but public schedules and slide hosts do not add up to every meetup. His [Speaker Deck](https://speakerdeck.com/rdegges) profile counter says 29 decks. The public listing and the Atom feed expose 18 of the later ones, and older decks still resolve at their own URLs. Many of those decks never name an event.

A full date below is an event date from a schedule, a video description, or his own description of that delivery. "Slides published" is the Speaker Deck date when no event date was found. "Announced" is a pre-event agenda with no recording or recap. A month or a year means the source did not pin the day. Years with nothing here, including 2022 and 2025, are gaps in what could be documented. A December 3, 2025 LinkedIn post from The AI Security Summit says he kicked off a workshop. It does not give a session title, so it is not listed.

Sessionize also lists talk abstracts, including several Snyk and Atlassian security talks, with no event attached. Those abstracts are not in the timeline. Lanyrd shows him attending or tracking PyCon, NodeConf, Write the Docs, DjangoCon US 2011, and others. Attendance is not listed as a talk. Two talks he mentioned as upcoming on February 22, 2014, one at Rackspace and one at Rocket Space, were never confirmed afterward, so they are left out.
